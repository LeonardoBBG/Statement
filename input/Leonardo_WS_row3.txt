Escalation Only After Role Change (February 2024)
In February 2024, I applied for and was successful in obtaining a role in a different team within the same department. I did so following a strong recommendation from my then manager, Erika Oteri, who encouraged me to apply for the role and supported the move. At no point was I informed, nor could it reasonably be understood, that this move constituted a demotion, a reduction in seniority, or that my prior role and experience would thereafter be treated as equivalent to a junior position.
The role I moved into was titled Trade Desk Analyst. This followed my prior role as an Experienced Enterprise Relationship Service Representative, which was a senior role involving project delivery, client escalations, and acting as a technical and knowledge resource for the team. In substance and operation, the change represented a move from a senior, project- and escalation-led role into a junior, queue-centric role, notwithstanding how it was later characterised by the Respondent.
The Respondent has stated in its ET3 that the Trade Desk Analyst role required assignment to the queue in accordance with team rotas. That is correct. Queue and ticket handling were the primary responsibility of a Trade Desk Analyst. By contrast, queue throughput was not the primary function of my prior senior role, which was expressly structured around project delivery, client remediation, and escalation support. The responsibilities and performance expectations attached to those two roles were therefore materially different.
While I was performing the senior role of Experienced Enterprise Relationship Service Representative, adverse system behaviour was identified and discussed in July 2023 and was explained in technical terms. At that time, it was not escalated, restricted, or treated as a disciplinary issue. No instruction was given to alter my working practices, reduce or avoid environment switching, reprioritise queue activity, or curtail the project and escalation work I had been instructed to deliver as part of that senior role.
It was only after I moved into the Trade Desk Analyst role that the same system behaviour was escalated and treated as a disciplinary issue, notwithstanding that the underlying activities, technical causes, and system behaviour had not changed. This change in treatment followed the role change alone, not any change in conduct, working practices, or risk profile.
While performing my senior role, I had no motivation or incentive to avoid queue tickets, as queue throughput was not my primary responsibility and my performance was assessed principally by reference to project delivery, client escalation, and technical support. Notwithstanding that, the disciplinary process relied on system data and activity drawn from the period in which I was performing that senior role and retrospectively applied it against the expectations of a junior, queue-centric Trade Desk Analyst role.
The disciplinary process placed significant reliance on system crash statistics viewed in isolation. It failed to account for the materially different responsibilities attached to the two roles, the technical explanations already identified months earlier, the absence of any contemporaneous instruction or guidance, and the inconsistency inherent in judging senior-role activity by junior-role standards.
